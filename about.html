About Mikado Jiu Jitsu

From a dare to a dojo

Mikado Jiu Jitsu is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academy in DHA Phase 4, Lahore. Black belt Shaheryar Khalid opened it in May 2024 to fulfil a lifelong ambition: a proper jiu-jitsu school in Pakistan, open to everyone — beginners, competitors, women and children.

2014 – 2015

Before Mikado Jiu Jitsu: jiu-jitsu reaches Pakistan

A decade ago, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu was barely known in Pakistan. As a close-contact sport it has been slow to catch on across the region, and slower still among women.

The first organised events came in 2014 and 2015. The Pakistan Grappling Challenge in Lahore drew grapplers from Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Karachi and even Afghanistan, and UK-based black belt Yousuf Nabi came to teach a ten-day course — reported at the time as the first time a BJJ black belt had taught in Pakistan.

2011 – 2023

The founder’s road

Shaheryar’s own start was a dare. In 2011 a friend dared him to take a Pak Fight Club bout with no training at all. He lost, and found a purpose. By 2013 he was a jiu-jitsu white belt, and in 2015 he took silver in the open-weight division at the Pakistan Grappling Challenge.

He trained and competed in Malaysia, earned his purple belt in 2018, and in 2023 turned months out with an ACL injury into Grapplefest, his own league to give Pakistani athletes a well-run competition. That same year he was back competing against experienced black belts and teaching kids and adults at GFTeam Malaysia under Professor Pedro Falbo.

2024

Opening the doors

In March 2024 Professor Pedro Falbo awarded Shaheryar his black belt, almost twelve years after his first class. Two months later, in May 2024, he opened Mikado Jiu Jitsu in Lahore.

By August he was promoting his own students for the first time. Six months after opening, the whole Mikado Jiu Jitsu family — adults and kids, morning and evening members — filled the mats for one photo.

The challenges

What it took

Opening a jiu-jitsu academy in Pakistan meant three hurdles: cost, competition and culture.

  • Cost

    A proper academy means real start-up costs. Mikado Jiu Jitsu spent them where members notice: genuine, high-quality mats — “a rarity in Pakistani gyms”, as one visitor put it — and a room that is kept spotless.

  • Competition

    Lahore already had martial arts and MMA gyms. Mikado Jiu Jitsu set itself apart with structured classes — Fundamentals for beginners, Advanced for experienced grapplers — and a black belt coaching every level.

  • Culture

    A close-contact sport is a hard sell in Pakistan, especially for women and their families. Mikado Jiu Jitsu built a clean, secure room where women train and compete for the gym, kids train in their own age groups, and parents are welcome to stay and watch.

It worked. Mikado Jiu Jitsu reached break-even, and its story became the subject of a business case study by researchers at Birmingham City University, published in January 2026, on growing a sports academy against Pakistan’s societal norms.

Milestones

Mikado Jiu Jitsu so far

  1. May 2024

    Mikado Jiu Jitsu opens in DHA Phase 4, Lahore.

  2. August 2024

    Shaheryar promotes his own students for the first time.

  3. July 2025

    UK-based black belt Naqi Arshed teaches guard passing in a donation-based seminar, with every rupee going to support the athlete.

  4. November 2025

    2025 IBJJF Grand Slam champion Zain Chaudhry teaches the berimbolo. All proceeds go towards building a water well in rural Sindh.

  5. January 2026

    Mikado Jiu Jitsu’s growth is published as a business case study by Birmingham City University researchers.

  6. February 2026

    Shaheryar goes four from four and takes double gold.

  7. June 2026

    Professor Alisha Ahmad, a pioneer of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu in Pakistan and founder of Carlson Gracie Karachi, teaches spider guard at Mikado Jiu Jitsu.

  8. August 2026

    Mikado Jiu Jitsu becomes the first local jiu-jitsu gym from Pakistan to compete abroad as a team. At Grappling Industries Kuala Lumpur, 9 adults and 2 kids finish second of 61 teams, with 7 gold, 12 silver and 2 bronze medals. Shaheryar makes his black belt debut and takes silver.

  9. August 2026

    Serr Janjua, black belt and BJJ professor at SiO Oslo, teaches a seminar to give back to the Pakistani BJJ community.

  10. October 2026

    Full circle: Yousuf Nabi — the black belt who taught that first course in Pakistan more than a decade ago — teaches a seminar at Mikado Jiu Jitsu.

“There’s no secret sauce overseas that magically makes training better. It’s fundamentals, consistency, and how coaches structure their classes. That’s what really matters.”

Shaheryar Khalid, 2026

What’s next

A Mikado Jiu Jitsu in every city

Shaheryar’s aim is at least one school under the Mikado Jiu Jitsu name in every major city in Pakistan — so that the next kid who walks in on a dare finds good jiu-jitsu close to home.
